# Task 4 – Development Methodology and Why It Was Selected
## Mobile Shop Management System

### 1. Introduction
A system development methodology is an organized approach used to plan, analyze, design, build, test, and deliver a software system. Selecting an appropriate methodology helps the team control project activities and respond to changes.

### 2. Selected Methodology: Agile (Iterative and Incremental)
The team proposes using the **Agile methodology**, with work divided into short iterations. At the end of each iteration, the team reviews the completed work, receives feedback, and updates the next priorities.

### 3. How Agile Will Be Applied
The proposed work will be organized as follows:

1. **Requirements and planning:** Identify users, business problems, and the required system features.
2. **Iteration planning:** Select a small group of features for the next development cycle.
3. **Design:** Prepare the required interface, database structure, and workflow.
4. **Development:** Implement the selected features.
5. **Testing:** Check the features and correct discovered problems.
6. **Review and feedback:** Demonstrate the current version to the shop representative or supervisor and collect comments.
7. **Next iteration:** Prioritize improvements and continue until the agreed scope is completed.

### 4. Proposed Iterations

| Iteration | Main focus | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| Iteration 1 | Product and category management | Product records and search |
| Iteration 2 | Inventory management | Stock quantities and low-stock visibility |
| Iteration 3 | Sales management | Sales recording and stock updates |
| Iteration 4 | Suppliers, purchases, and reports | Purchase records and basic reports |
| Iteration 5 | Integration, testing, and refinement | Reviewed and corrected system version |

The iteration plan is preliminary and may be adjusted after requirements are confirmed.

### 5. Why Agile Was Selected
Agile is suitable for this project for the following reasons:

- **Changing requirements:** The shop owner may request changes after seeing an early version.
- **Early feedback:** Users can review features before the whole system is completed.
- **Incremental delivery:** Important functions can be developed and checked in manageable parts.
- **Team collaboration:** Tasks can be divided among team members according to their responsibilities.
- **Earlier problem discovery:** Testing each iteration can reveal errors before they affect the entire system.
- **Progress visibility:** The team can track completed, ongoing, and pending work.

### 6. Comparison with Other Methodologies

| Methodology | Main characteristic | Suitability for this project |
|---|---|---|
| Waterfall | Sequential phases; each phase is generally completed before the next | Less flexible when shop requirements change |
| Agile | Short iterations with continuous feedback | High; supports feedback and gradual development |
| Spiral | Repeated cycles with strong risk analysis | Useful for large, high-risk projects, but may be complex for this small system |

### 7. Limitations and How They Will Be Managed
Agile requires frequent communication and clear priorities. Changes can also affect the schedule if they are not controlled. The team will manage these risks by:
- Holding short progress meetings.
- Recording approved requirements and changes.
- Assigning one owner to each task.
- Testing completed features before accepting them.
- Reviewing the schedule when a major change is approved.

### 8. Conclusion
Agile is the proposed methodology because it provides flexibility, frequent feedback, incremental progress, and clear opportunities for testing. These characteristics match a small shop-management project whose users may refine their needs while reviewing the system.

### 9. Current Project Status
Agile is the selected proposed approach for the project. The iterations above describe the planned development process; they do not mean that all listed features have already been implemented.
