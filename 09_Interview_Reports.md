# Interview Report 1 – Shop Owner / Manager

### A. Interview Information

- **Interviewee ID:** INT-01
- **Role:** Shop Owner / Manager
- **Date:** To be completed after the real interview
- **Duration:** Approximately 10–15 minutes
- **Objective:** Understand management needs and the main business problems.

### B. Questions and Illustrative Answers

**Q1. How do you currently manage products and their prices?**  
Illustrative answer: Product details and prices are currently recorded manually or in separate files. This can make it difficult to find updated information quickly.

**Q2. What are the most common problems you face when managing the shop?**  
Illustrative answer: The main problems are difficulty tracking stock, finding old sales information, and avoiding mistakes in manual records.

**Q3. How do you know which products need to be reordered?**  
Illustrative answer: Stock is checked manually. A product may run out before the need to reorder is noticed.

**Q4. What reports would help you manage the shop?**  
Illustrative answer: Daily and monthly sales reports, current stock reports, low-stock alerts, and profit summaries would be useful.

**Q5. Who should be allowed to access the system?**  
Illustrative answer: The owner or manager should have full access, while employees should only access the functions needed for their jobs.

### C. Key Findings

1. Management needs a central place for product, sales, and stock information.
2. Reports should help the owner monitor sales and inventory.
3. Access permissions should differ according to the user's role.
4. Low-stock notifications could reduce the risk of running out of products.

### D. Requirements Identified

- The system should allow authorized users to add, edit, search, and view products.
- The system should record sales and update stock quantities.
- The system should provide sales and inventory reports.
- The system should support role-based access.

### E. Conclusion

The illustrative interview indicates that the owner needs accurate, centralized information and clear reports to support daily decisions. These findings should be confirmed with the actual shop owner.

# Interview Report 2 – Sales Employee

### A. Interview Information

- **Interviewee ID:** INT-02
- **Role:** Sales Employee
- **Date:** To be completed after the real interview
- **Duration:** Approximately 10–15 minutes
- **Objective:** Understand the sales process and employee needs.

### B. Questions and Illustrative Answers

**Q1. How do you search for a product when a customer asks about it?**  
Illustrative answer: The employee checks the shelves or asks another employee. This can take time, especially when the shop has many models or product types.

**Q2. What information do you need before completing a sale?**  
Illustrative answer: Product name, model, price, available quantity, and any relevant product details.

**Q3. What mistakes can happen during a manual sale?**  
Illustrative answer: The wrong price or quantity may be written down, and stock records may not be updated immediately.

**Q4. Would a computerized search screen help you? Why?**  
Illustrative answer: Yes. Searching by product name or model would make it faster to answer customers and check availability.

**Q5. What should a sales screen be like?**  
Illustrative answer: It should be simple, clear, and require only a few steps to find a product and record a sale.

### C. Key Findings

1. Product searching should be quick and straightforward.
2. Employees need to see accurate prices and available quantities.
3. Sales entry should be simple to reduce mistakes.
4. The interface should be easy to learn.

### D. Requirements Identified

- The system should provide product search by name or model.
- The system should display price and available quantity.
- The system should allow authorized employees to record sales.
- The system should reduce stock automatically after a completed sale.

### E. Conclusion

The illustrative interview highlights the importance of a simple sales interface and fast product search. These findings should be confirmed with an actual sales employee.

# Interview Report 3 – Inventory / Cashier Employee

### A. Interview Information

- **Interviewee ID:** INT-03
- **Role:** Inventory / Cashier Employee
- **Date:** To be completed after the real interview
- **Duration:** Approximately 10–15 minutes
- **Objective:** Understand stock control, purchasing, and payment-recording activities.

### B. Questions and Illustrative Answers

**Q1. How do you currently record products received by the shop?**  
Illustrative answer: New stock is counted and then recorded manually. Updating records can be delayed when the shop is busy.

**Q2. How do you track the quantity of each product?**  
Illustrative answer: Quantities are checked using written records or spreadsheets and sometimes by counting the physical stock.

**Q3. What happens when the recorded quantity does not match the physical quantity?**  
Illustrative answer: Employees recount the product and review previous sales or stock entries to find the difference.

**Q4. What information should be saved for each sale or payment?**  
Illustrative answer: Date, products, quantities, total amount, payment method, and the employee who recorded the transaction.

**Q5. What would make inventory work easier?**  
Illustrative answer: Automatic stock updates, a low-stock warning, and a report showing current quantities would help.

### C. Key Findings

1. Stock records should be updated whenever products are received or sold.
2. The system should make it easy to review stock quantities.
3. Sales and payment information should be recorded consistently.
4. Low-stock alerts and stock reports would support inventory control.

### D. Requirements Identified

- The system should record incoming stock.
- The system should update quantities after sales.
- The system should show current stock levels.
- The system should provide low-stock notifications or indicators.
- The system should store transaction details for later review.

### E. Conclusion

The illustrative interview suggests that reliable stock updates and transaction records are important for daily operations. The findings should be validated with the actual employee responsible for inventory or cashier duties.

## 2. Overall Interview Analysis

Across the three illustrative roles, the common needs are:

1. Centralized product and inventory information.
2. Fast product search and accurate prices.
3. Recording sales and updating stock automatically.
4. Sales and inventory reports.
5. Simple screens and appropriate user permissions.
