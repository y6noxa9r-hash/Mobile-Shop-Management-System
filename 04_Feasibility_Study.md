# Task 2 – Feasibility Study
## Mobile Shop Management System

### 1. Introduction
This feasibility study evaluates whether the proposed Mobile Shop Management System can be developed and used successfully in a mobile-phone retail shop. It considers technical, economic, operational, schedule, and legal feasibility before the project proceeds to detailed design and implementation.

### 2. Technical Feasibility
The system is technically feasible because it can be developed using commonly available technologies and standard computer equipment.

- **Hardware:** A desktop or laptop computer, with a barcode scanner and receipt printer as optional devices.
- **Software:** A database management system, a programming language, and a user interface framework can be selected according to the development team's skills.
- **Network:** A local network or reliable internet connection may be used depending on whether the system is deployed locally or online.
- **Technical skills:** The project team can divide the work into analysis, interface design, database design, implementation, and testing.

**Conclusion:** The required technology is available and the system does not depend on unusual or experimental equipment. The final technology stack will be confirmed during design.

### 3. Economic Feasibility
The project is expected to provide value by reducing manual work, improving stock accuracy, speeding up sales transactions, and making reports easier to prepare.

Potential costs include:
- Development and testing effort.
- Computer equipment or upgrades, if required.
- Optional barcode scanner and receipt printer.
- Hosting, backup, or maintenance costs, depending on deployment.

Potential benefits include:
- Less time spent recording sales and checking stock.
- Fewer calculation and data-entry errors.
- Better visibility of products and low-stock items.
- Faster access to sales and purchase reports.
- Better-informed purchasing decisions.

A preliminary financial estimate is provided in **Task 3 – Cash Flow**. The figures are planning assumptions, not supplier quotations or confirmed shop expenses.

**Conclusion:** The project is economically promising if the value of time saved and improved stock control exceeds the final implementation and operating costs. Actual costs must be confirmed before purchasing equipment or services.

### 4. Operational Feasibility
The system supports the shop's normal activities and is intended for:
- Shop owner or manager.
- Sales employees.
- Inventory employees.
- System administrator, if required.

The interface should be simple and require only basic training. Employees will be able to search for products, record sales, update stock, and review relevant information according to their permissions. The system should not interrupt shop operations during the transition; initial data can be entered and checked before full use.

**Conclusion:** The system is operationally feasible provided that employees receive short training, responsibilities are clear, and data is entered consistently.

### 5. Schedule Feasibility
A preliminary eight-week schedule is proposed:

| Period | Main activity |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Problem definition and requirements confirmation |
| Week 2 | Feasibility review and project planning |
| Week 3 | System and database design |
| Weeks 4–5 | Core implementation |
| Week 6 | Complete remaining modules and integration |
| Week 7 | Testing and corrections |
| Week 8 | User review, documentation, and delivery preparation |

The schedule is achievable for a small academic project if team members complete their assigned work and review progress regularly. Delays in requirements approval or integration may affect the delivery date.

**Conclusion:** The proposed schedule is feasible as an initial estimate and will be updated when the project scope and team availability are confirmed.

### 6. Legal and Security Feasibility
The system should:
- Store only business information needed for shop operations.
- Restrict access through individual accounts and role-based permissions.
- Protect passwords and prevent unauthorized access.
- Keep backups and limit access to customer information, if any is collected.
- Use properly licensed software and respect applicable data-protection requirements.

**Conclusion:** No legal obstacle is expected for the proposed business system, provided that the shop follows applicable Egyptian laws and uses software and data responsibly. Specific compliance requirements should be checked before deployment.

### 7. Overall Feasibility Result
| Feasibility area | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | Feasible | Standard hardware and software are sufficient |
| Economic | Promising; needs cost confirmation | Expected operational benefits may justify the costs |
| Operational | Feasible | Fits the shop's daily activities with basic training |
| Schedule | Feasible as a preliminary plan | Eight-week plan with manageable phases |
| Legal and security | Feasible with controls | Requires access control, backups, and lawful data handling |

### 8. Final Recommendation
The Mobile Shop Management System should proceed to the detailed analysis and design stage. The project is feasible in principle, while the financial figures, exact technology choices, and deployment requirements should be validated with the shop owner before implementation.

### 9. Current Project Status
The team is working through the planning and analysis deliverables. This study is a preliminary assessment and does not claim that the system has already been implemented or deployed.
